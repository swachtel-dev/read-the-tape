A stock's price is not handed down from on high. It is the running result of a tug-of-war: at any instant, the price is wherever buyers and sellers agree to trade. That constant negotiation has a name, price discovery, and it is what this game is about.

Here is the one idea underneath every round: a price moves most when someone is forced to trade or the eager crowd runs out. A trader who bet against a stock may be forced to buy it back. An index fund may be forced to buy a stock the day it joins the index. A crowd that already piled in has no one left to sell to. Read who must trade and who is spent, and you can often tell which way the price breaks, whatever the headline says.

Each round drops you into a concrete situation with a few plain facts. You make one clear call, then the price plays out over the next few days and you see why it moved. No finance background needed. Every new term is explained the first time it appears, so hover, tap, or keyboard-focus any word with a dotted underline. Try it now on price discovery and short squeeze, two ideas you will use right away.

    What am I looking at? Every part, in plain words
    The headline and facts
    A real-world situation from the stock market. The facts are the clues that tell you which way pressure is building. Read them for who is forced to trade and who is out of ammunition.
    Your position
    What you already own going into the round. Usually you start flat, meaning you hold none of the stock. In one round you start holding a big winner and must decide what to do with it.
    The tape
    A one-line read of what the buying and selling look like right now. On a real desk, watching the tape means watching orders flow past in real time.
    Your call
    Pick one action. Buy bets the price rises. Short bets it falls. Sit out risks nothing. When you hold a position you can Hold, Sell, or Short instead.
    Conviction (size)
    How much money you put behind a directional call. Big doubles the swing, up or down. It only matters when you take a real position, and going big is not always smart.
    Lock in the call
    Commits your decision and reveals the next five days of trading. You cannot change your mind once the tape starts printing.
